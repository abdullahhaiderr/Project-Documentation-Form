# Client message, requirements prompt and AI scoping prompt

Three things the studio can reuse with every new client:

1. A ready-to-send email (and a shorter WhatsApp version) that introduces the
   Project Brief and sets expectations.
2. A plain-language requirements prompt, grouped by project type, that a
   non-technical owner can answer in one sitting.
3. A copy-paste AI prompt that turns a client's answers into a scoped project plan
   and quote outline.

Replace everything in `[square brackets]` and `{{double braces}}` before sending.
British English throughout. No emoji.

---

## 1. Introduction email

**Subject:** Your project brief - next step

```text
Hello [First name],

Thank you for getting in touch about [one-line description of the project]. Before
we talk numbers, I would like to understand your business and what you need, so I
have put together a short Project Brief for you to complete.

[Link to the Project Brief]

It takes about 15 to 20 minutes. Answer what you can and skip anything you are
unsure about - we can fill the gaps on a call. The brief covers your goals, your
audience, the pages and features you need, your brand and colours, and your
timeline and budget. At the end you can download a PDF copy of your answers for
your records.

What happens next:

1. You send the brief.
2. We review it and reply within two working days.
3. If anything is unclear, we will send a few questions or suggest a short call.
4. You receive a written scope and quote.

If you would rather talk first, just reply to this email or message me on
[phone number] and we will arrange a time.

Warm regards,
[Your name]
Creative Haider
contact.abdullahhaider@gmail.com
[website or phone, optional]
```

### Short WhatsApp version

```text
Hello [First name], thanks for your message about [project]. To quote accurately,
could you complete our short Project Brief? It takes 15-20 minutes and you can
download a copy at the end: [link]. If you would rather talk it through first,
just say and we will arrange a call. - [Your name], Creative Haider
```

### Before you send

- [ ] The form link opens on a phone and on a desktop.
- [ ] The link is the public `https://` URL, not a local file path.
- [ ] The reply window is realistic for the studio's workload.
- [ ] The client's name and project description are filled in.
- [ ] The tone matches the client: keep this version for new enquiries, and trim it
      further for clients who already know the studio.

## 2. Client requirements prompt

Use this when a client prefers not to use the form, or wants to prepare before they
start. Send it as a plain-text list or paste it into a shared document. It is
deliberately shorter than the form: it gives the studio the story and the
constraints, while the form captures the detail.

**Message to accompany it:**

> If you would rather prepare in your own words, or answer before opening the form,
> these are the key questions. A sentence or two each is plenty. Short answers are
> fine, and "not sure yet" is a valid answer.

### A. Your business

1. What does your business do, in one or two sentences?
2. Who is your ideal customer - age, location, and what they need?
3. What are you known for, and what makes you different from others doing similar
   work?
4. How long have you been running, and how many people are on the team?
5. Which services or products bring you the most revenue today?
6. Who will be our main contact, and who signs off the final work?

### B. The project

1. What do you want us to build or do? Describe it in your own words.
2. Why now - what has changed, or what problem is this solving?
3. What should a visitor or user do first?
4. What three things would make this project a success?
5. Have you tried anything before that worked, or did not work?
6. Are there existing websites, systems or accounts this needs to fit with?

### C. Your project type

Ask the client to answer **one block** - or more than one if the project overlaps.

#### Website or landing page

1. Which pages do you expect? (for example: Home, About, Services, Case studies,
   Blog, Contact)
2. What content already exists, and what needs writing from scratch?
3. Do you have a logo and brand colours, or do you need those too?
4. Does it need anything beyond pages - for example booking, payments, a login
   area, search or a blog?
5. Will you update the site yourself after launch?
6. Are there websites you like or dislike, and why?

#### Online shop

1. What will you sell, roughly how many products, and where do you ship?
2. How do you take payment today, and with which provider?
3. What is already decided about delivery, returns, tax and stock?
4. Do you need customer accounts, reviews, discounts or subscriptions?
5. Who handles orders and customer emails day to day?

#### Web app, portal or dashboard

1. Who will use it, and what are the different roles?
2. What three things must it do on day one?
3. What can wait for a later version?
4. Which systems or data does it need to connect to? For example a CRM,
   spreadsheet, payment provider or accounting tool.
5. What happens today without it - spreadsheets, email, manual work?
6. Roughly how many users do you expect in the first year?

#### Mobile app

1. iPhone, Android or both?
2. What is the one core action a user takes?
3. Does it need to work offline, send notifications, or use the camera or location?
4. Do you already have an app store account?
5. Who will maintain it after launch?

#### Automation or integration

1. Which tasks take the most time each week?
2. Walk through one task step by step, from start to finish.
3. Which tools are involved, and can they connect to other software? For example,
   do they offer an API or a built-in integration?
4. What should trigger the automation, and what should happen at the end?
5. How often does it run, and what should happen if it fails?

#### Social media management

1. Which platforms matter, and why?
2. What should people do as a result - follow, enquire, buy?
3. Who creates the photos and videos now?
4. How often do you want to post?
5. Do you want paid ads as well, or organic content only?

#### Branding or graphic design

1. What do you need - logo, brand guidelines, print, packaging, social templates?
2. How should the brand feel, in three words?
3. Are there colours, fonts or styles you want, or want to avoid?
4. Where will the brand be used most - web, print, signage, packaging?
5. Who else needs to approve it?

### D. Content, brand and visuals

1. Do you have a logo and brand colours? Files are ideal.
2. Do you have professional photos, or should we plan a shoot or use stock?
3. What tone should the writing have - formal, friendly, technical, direct?
4. Are there phrases, claims or legal wording we must use or avoid?
5. Which languages does it need? If any language is written right to left (for
   example Urdu or Arabic), say so.

### E. Timeline, budget and decisions

1. When would you like this live, and is there a date that cannot move?
2. What budget range feels comfortable? A rough range is enough; if you are unsure,
   tell us what you have spent on similar work before.
3. How many rounds of feedback do you expect, and who else is involved?
4. Are there fixed costs you already know about - hosting, licences, stock images,
   payment fees?
5. What has to be true for you to sign off?
6. Do you want ongoing support after launch - updates, backups, content changes?

### F. Anything else

1. Is there anything we should know that has not been asked?
2. What is the best way to reach you, and when?

**Closing line for the client:**

> You do not need to answer everything perfectly. We use these answers to prepare
> questions and a scope, not to hold you to fixed requirements.

## 3. AI scoping prompt

Paste this into an AI assistant together with the client's answers. It produces a
plan and quote outline, not a final contract.

```text
You are a senior project lead at Creative Haider, a small design and development
studio (contact.abdullahhaider@gmail.com). Your job is to turn a client's brief into
a clear, realistic project plan and quote outline.

INPUTS
- Client name: {{CLIENT_NAME}}
- Business name: {{BUSINESS_NAME}}
- Project type: {{PROJECT_TYPE}}
- Currency: {{CURRENCY}}
- Studio rate card or day rate: {{RATE_CARD}}
- Typical timeline for this project type: {{TYPICAL_TIMELINE}}
- Client's answers: {{CLIENT_ANSWERS}}

TASK
Produce a scoping pack with exactly these sections:

1. Snapshot - five bullets: who the client is, what they need, why now, the main
   constraint, the main opportunity.
2. Goals and success measures - what the project should achieve and how both sides
   will know it worked.
3. Scope - included. A numbered list of deliverables, one line each.
4. Scope - not included. Explicit exclusions and assumptions, for example
   copywriting, photography, paid ads, third-party fees, content migration.
5. Plan and milestones. A table: phase, what happens, client input needed,
   approximate duration. Use a realistic sequence and flag dependencies.
6. Client responsibilities. What the client must provide and by when: content,
   feedback, approvals, access.
7. Estimate. An effort table in days per phase, an optional range in {{CURRENCY}}
   based on {{RATE_CARD}}, and separately priced optional extras. Give ranges,
   never single figures.
8. Risks and unknowns. Ranked, each with a mitigation or a question to ask.
9. Questions before quoting. No more than eight, ordered by how much they change
   the estimate.
10. Client summary email. Under 150 words, warm and plain, no jargon, confirming
    next steps.

RULES
- Use British English. Plain language, short sentences.
- Never invent budget, timeline, legal, technical or market facts. If something is
  missing, list it as an assumption or a question.
- Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves, and flag anything likely to become a
  change request.
- Identify any paid third-party service the project depends on (hosting, payment
  fees, stock images, app store fees, email tools) and say so explicitly.
- Present ranges and conditions, never a fixed price or a promised date.
- If the answers conflict, say so and ask which takes priority.
```

### Placeholders

| Placeholder | What to paste |
|---|---|
| `{{CLIENT_NAME}}` | Client's full name |
| `{{BUSINESS_NAME}}` | Trading name, or "not yet trading" |
| `{{PROJECT_TYPE}}` | Website, shop, web app, mobile app, automation, social media, branding, or a combination |
| `{{CURRENCY}}` | e.g. `PKR`, `GBP`, `USD`, `AED` |
| `{{RATE_CARD}}` | The studio's day rate and any fixed prices it is willing to share with the assistant |
| `{{TYPICAL_TIMELINE}}` | e.g. "4-6 weeks for a five-page website" |
| `{{CLIENT_ANSWERS}}` | The completed brief, the section 2 answers, or both |

### How to use it

1. Paste the prompt and fill every placeholder. Do not leave `{{...}}` in place.
2. Add the client's answers in full, including the parts that seem unimportant;
   the assistant needs them to spot exclusions and risks.
3. Review the output against the studio's own judgement. Treat every estimate as a
   draft that a human prices.
4. Send only the client summary email (section 10), after editing it to match the
   studio's voice and confirming the numbers internally.
5. Keep the full pack for the internal scope and quote; do not send it to the
   client unedited.

### Review checklist before a quote leaves the studio

- [ ] Every must-have in the client's answers appears in the scope.
- [ ] Exclusions are listed, especially content, photography and third-party fees.
- [ ] The estimate is a range, and the low end is still profitable.
- [ ] Unknowns are turned into questions, not silently priced.
- [ ] Timeline includes client feedback and approval time.
- [ ] Any third-party cost is flagged as the client's cost or quoted separately.
- [ ] The summary email matches the internal scope.
